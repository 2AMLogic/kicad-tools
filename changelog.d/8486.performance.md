- **`kct route` stops re-searching stalled unsealed nets at the full per-net cap every iteration** (Issue #8486).
  On a zero-overflow-stalled board the negotiated loop searched each still-unrouted net twice per
  iteration — the zero-overflow recovery and the targeted-ripup fallback — and neither search fed the
  #8486 known-failure back-off, so the reported narrow signal nets kept the full ~30 s cap each time
  and only one ever backed off. Both searches now back off while the foreign copper seen at the
  first failure remains in place under the same clearance rules. Extra copper can come and go.
  Removing a blocking track or via, or transferring it to a net whose clearance opens a path,
  restores the full budget on the next search, including its Python fallback if C++ cannot find a path.
  This covers detours beyond the terminals’ bounding box.
  Every three iterations the router also retries at the full budget. Failed nets stay
  in the failed-nets report and the per-iteration known-fail seconds line now counts these searches.
- **`kct route` skips provably hopeless escape-fence searches** (Issue #8486). When every
  legal goal cell of a fenced connection is covered by another net's hard trace fence, the
  fenced A* can never accept a goal, so the router now runs the unfenced search directly
  with the full budget instead of first draining the fenced one. On the public
  forced-recovery fixture this removes the whole duplicated first budget (measured 1.8 s of
  1.9 s per connection with no deadline; the full 0.05-2 s cap with one) while producing the
  identical route; connections whose goal is not fully fence-covered keep the fenced search
  and its lane-keeping shape unchanged.
